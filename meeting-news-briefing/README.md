# Meeting News Briefing

The news that matters before a meeting is rarely the loudest story. It is the one that changes how you should walk into the room.

This skill checks recent public news about the companies you are meeting with and gives you a short, cited briefing before the meeting happens.

## What you get

For each company, it gives you:

- the one to three developments most likely to matter;
- why they may change the tone, timing or priorities in the room;
- a few practical questions or preparation prompts; and
- direct links to the sources.

A large redundancy round, leadership change, security incident or unexpected set of results is useful context. A vaguely related press release from three weeks ago is not. The skill is deliberately picky.

If there is no material recent news, it says so instead of filling the page.

## How to use it

Give it the company names and meeting dates, or let it read an authorised calendar if your AI assistant supports that.

Run it manually before an important meeting, or schedule it once a week so the briefing arrives before you remember to ask for it.

A good starting instruction is:

> Brief me on material news about the companies I am meeting with in the next seven days.

## How to install it

**Claude Code** — copy the SKILL.md file into .claude/skills/meeting-news-briefing/SKILL.md in your project or home directory.

**Slack** — open SKILL.md and paste its contents in as a Slackbot skill.

**Anything else** — paste the contents of SKILL.md into an AI assistant that can search the public web. Calendar access is useful but optional.

Scheduling is handled by whichever workflow or automation tool you use. The skill decides what belongs in the briefing; the schedule decides when it runs.

## The rules that matter

It cites every concrete news claim, separates facts from inference, and does not guess how a specific meeting attendee feels.

It also keeps confidential notes, private attendee details and your relationship with the company out of web searches.

## Make it yours

Change the lookback window, the kinds of developments you care about, or the briefing format. Keep the accuracy and privacy rules intact.
